NOT EVERY IMPRESSION
LEAVES AN IMPRESSION.

LOOP Digital is a premium media partner. We decide where a brand appears — which environments, which audiences, which moments — because placement shapes perception. The brand has to do the same job the media does: be seen immediately, be understood in a second, and be unmistakable the next time it appears.

Everything in this book follows from that. Maximum weight, minimum decoration, one loud accent held back until it matters.

01 — THE OO IS THE ASSET
The name already holds a matched pair of round counters. That pair is the symbol, and it becomes the brand emoji: the same two counters read as eyes, with a mouth drawn between them. No external graphic element is ever added.
02 — INK FIRST, ACCENT LAST
Near-black carries 85% of every surface. Acid is a signal, not a background.
03 — ONE VOICE, FIVE SIZES
One family, five steps, no exceptions. The headline is built like the logotype.

THIS IS THE
LOOP EMOJI.

The agency's own emoji: the logotype's two counters become the eyes, an acid arc becomes the mouth. It is how LOOP signs a message, reacts in a thread and closes a film — the logotype speaks, the emoji smiles.

TWO COLOURS, ALWAYS BOTH

Neon violet for the eyes, acid yellow for the smile, on an ink ground. Neither colour ever appears alone — the contrast is what makes it read as a face instead of a logo fragment.

GEOMETRY
The eyes are the logotype’s own counters at full stroke. The mouth is a single arc, never a closed shape.
ONE FACE PER FRAME
Never two expressions in the same layout, and never beside the full lockup — the emoji replaces it.
MINIMUM SIZE
24 px. Below that the arc thins out and the face reads as three dots.

THE FACE
HOLDS THE RAMP.

The ramps are backgrounds, not brand colour — so the emoji has to survive all four of them. It does, because it is only two shapes in one colour: the face stays white and the ramp does the rest.

On a ramp the emoji is monochrome white — never split into two accent colours. Place it over the upper half of the ramp, where the deeper tones keep the arc’s edge.

Neon violet
The most premium of the five and the least used in media. Night-time, cinematic. Loses power in CMYK and needs a dark ground to hold.

#FF2D9E
Neon fuchsia
Fashion-forward and highly distinctive. Poor contrast against white type, unreliable in print, and it dates faster than the others.
VISIBILITY ●●●○○ · OWNABILITY ●●●●○
#2FA8FF
Neon blue
The most trusted and the most common. Reads as technology and utility. Safe on light and dark alike — and the least memorable.

THE COUNTER SWAP
UT OF HME
Any round counter in a display line may be redrawn as the logotype’s open ellipse. One swap per headline — two turns it into decoration.
THE RULETwo lines maximum, caps, each line scaled so both end on the same edge — the construction that holds LOOP over DIGITAL.
WHEN NOT TONever three lines, never centred, never over photography without a solid ground. If a line will not justify, it is too long — cut it.
IN MOTIONThe lines set their measure first, then the counter opens. Nothing rotates, nothing scales, nothing glows.
